## Structure

1. **Container**: `<span class="uoa-badge">` with short text — one or two words.
2. **Variant**: `--info | --success | --warning | --danger`; without a class it is neutral.

## Guidelines

### When to use

- For status next to a heading, card or list row: new, open, cancelled.
- For deadlines and numbers that must stand out at a glance.
- Inside filters and lists with many entries.

### When not to use

- For messages that need a sentence: use an alert.
- For actions: use a button or link.
- For more than two or three badges together: they stop standing out.

### Variants

| Variant | Class | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Neutral | `.uoa-badge` | General highlight, the default |
| Info | `.uoa-badge--info` | Neutral information with a date or number |
| Success | `.uoa-badge--success` | Something open, approved, available |
| Warning | `.uoa-badge--warning` | Something expiring or running out |
| Danger | `.uoa-badge--danger` | Something cancelled or failed |

### Content

- One or two words: "New", "Enrolment open", not sentences.
- The words state the status on their own — colour alone is not enough.

### Behaviour

- Static: no JavaScript, no interaction. It is not a button — don't wrap actions in a badge.
- Never wraps onto two lines (`white-space: nowrap`).

## Accessibility

- A badge is plain text in the flow: read with the page, no roles.
- Don't rely on colour alone: the words must say whether it's an error, a warning or success.
- Text colours meet at least 4.5:1 on their backgrounds (same pairs as the alert).
- In Windows High Contrast mode the `currentColor` border keeps the shape, since backgrounds are removed.
- Screen-reader testing (NVDA, VoiceOver) hasn't been done yet.
